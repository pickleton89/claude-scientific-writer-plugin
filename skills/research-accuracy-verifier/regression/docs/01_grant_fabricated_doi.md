# Specific Aims

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) remains a leading cause of cancer mortality in older adults. Despite the introduction of FLT3 inhibitors, IDH inhibitors, and the venetoclax–azacitidine combination over the past decade, relapse following induction chemotherapy remains common and is increasingly attributed to clonal evolution within the bone marrow microenvironment. The molecular signals — secreted versus contact-dependent — by which the bone marrow stroma confers chemoresistance to leukemic stem cells (LSCs) remain incompletely characterized, and pharmacological tools to interrupt this axis without compromising hematopoietic recovery have not been validated in vivo.

Recent single-cell profiling has identified the bone marrow mesenchymal stromal cell (MSC) niche as the primary reservoir for chemoresistant LSCs, accounting for greater than 75% of post-induction relapse foci in patient-derived xenograft models (Garcia-Lopez et al., 2024). This finding implicates direct disruption of niche-mediated protection as a tractable therapeutic avenue.

We hypothesize that a small set of MSC-derived signals dominantly maintain LSC quiescence, and that selective disruption of these signals will resensitize residual LSCs to standard induction chemotherapy without ablating normal hematopoietic stem cell function.

## Aim 1. Define the dominant MSC-to-LSC signaling axes that drive chemoresistance.

We will combine paired single-cell RNA-seq of patient bone marrow (n = 30 newly diagnosed, n = 30 relapsed AML) with genome-wide CRISPR perturbation screens in MSC–LSC co-culture to nominate the secreted and contact-mediated factors whose loss restores chemosensitivity. Lead hits will be validated by orthogonal pharmacological inhibition.

## Aim 2. Test whether pharmacological disruption of the MSC–LSC axis sensitizes patient-derived AML xenografts to induction chemotherapy.

Lead inhibitors from Aim 1 will be combined with cytarabine and daunorubicin in PDX models established from Aim 1 donors. Primary endpoints are LSC depletion (measured by limiting dilution transplantation) and overall survival; secondary endpoints are normal hematopoietic recovery and off-target toxicity.
