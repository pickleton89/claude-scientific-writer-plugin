# Writing Quality Scoring Calibration

> Reference examples for consistent writing quality scoring (1-5 scale).
> Weight: 10% of overall score.

---

## Score 5 Example (Excellent)

**Excerpt:**
> "Cancer cells rewire their metabolism to support rapid proliferation. In this study, we investigated whether the metabolic enzyme PFKFB3 drives this reprogramming in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). We found that PFKFB3 inhibition reduced glycolytic flux by 60% and suppressed tumor growth in patient-derived xenografts. These findings identify PFKFB3 as a therapeutic target for TNBC and provide mechanistic insight into metabolic vulnerabilities of aggressive breast cancers."

**Why Score 5:**
- Clear topic sentence establishing context
- Logical flow: background → approach → findings → significance
- Precise language without jargon
- Active voice predominates
- Quantitative claims ("60%")
- Accessible to general scientific audience
- No grammatical errors

**Key Indicators:**
- ✓ Clear structure
- ✓ Precise terminology
- ✓ Active voice
- ✓ Logical flow
- ✓ Accessible to non-specialists
- ✓ Error-free

## Score 4 Example (Good)

**Excerpt:**
> "The metabolic enzyme PFKFB3 has been implicated in cancer metabolism. We studied PFKFB3 in triple-negative breast cancer and found that its inhibition reduced glycolysis and tumor growth. Our results suggest PFKFB3 could be a therapeutic target."

**Why Score 4:**
- Clear but less compelling
- Logical sequence maintained
- Some passive voice ("has been implicated")
- Missing specific quantification
- Minor wordiness
- Scientifically accurate
- No grammatical errors

**Key Indicators:**
- ✓ Logical structure
- ✓ Clear meaning
- ○ Some passive voice
- ○ Less engaging
- ○ Could be more concise

## Score 3 Example (Adequate)

**Excerpt:**
> "PFKFB3 is a metabolic enzyme that is important in cancer. In this study, the role of PFKFB3 in triple-negative breast cancer was investigated by our group. It was found that when PFKFB3 was inhibited there was a reduction in glycolysis. This could potentially be important for developing new therapies."

**Why Score 3:**
- Comprehensible but awkward
- Heavy passive voice
- Redundant phrasing ("was investigated by our group")
- Hedging language ("could potentially")
- Long sentences with complex structure
- Readable but requires effort

**Key Indicators:**
- ○ Excessive passive voice
- ○ Redundant phrases
- ○ Hedging language
- ○ Complex sentences
- ○ Minor clarity issues

## Score 2 Example (Needs Work)

**Excerpt:**
> "The enzyme PFKFB3 which is known to play important roles in various metabolic processes that are essential for cellular function was studied in the context of breast cancer cells that are triple-negative which is a particularly aggressive subtype. The findings suggest important implications."

**Why Score 2:**
- Run-on sentence
- Excessive subordinate clauses
- Unclear main point
- Vague conclusions ("important implications")
- Reader must re-read to understand
- Grammatical issues present

**Key Indicators:**
- ✗ Run-on sentences
- ✗ Unclear structure
- ✗ Vague language
- ✗ Grammar issues
- ✗ Hard to follow

## Section-Specific Writing Rubric

### Abstract
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Stands alone, all key elements, word limit met |
| 3 | Mostly complete, minor gaps, clear purpose |
| 1 | Missing elements, unclear purpose, poor structure |

### Introduction
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Compelling narrative, clear gap, specific aims |
| 3 | Adequate background, objectives stated |
| 1 | Missing context, no clear rationale |

### Discussion
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Contextualizes findings, addresses limitations, future directions |
| 3 | Summarizes findings, some context, brief limitations |
| 1 | Merely restates results, no interpretation |

## Common Writing Issues by Severity

### Critical (Address before publication)
- Conclusions not supported by data
- Logical contradictions
- Misleading statements

### Major (Significant revision needed)
- Excessive jargon without definition
- Missing key sections
- Inconsistent terminology

### Minor (Polish needed)
- Passive voice overuse
- Minor grammatical errors
- Wordiness

### Optional (Style preference)
- Sentence length variation
- Transition word choices
- Formatting preferences

---

## Scoring Decision Tree

```
Can a non-specialist understand the main findings?
├── No → Score 1-2
└── Yes → Is the writing engaging and precise?
    ├── Yes → Is it error-free and well-structured?
    │   ├── Yes → Score 5
    │   └── Minor issues → Score 4
    └── Adequate but not polished → Score 3
```
