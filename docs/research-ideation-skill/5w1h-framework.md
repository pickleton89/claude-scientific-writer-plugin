# 5W1H Framework for Research Ideation

## Overview

The 5W1H framework is a systematic thinking tool that helps researchers ideate and evaluate research ideas from multiple dimensions. By answering 6 key questions, you can quickly establish a comprehensive understanding of your research topic.

## Framework Components

### What (What)

**Core Question**: What problem or phenomenon will you research?

**Thinking Dimensions**:
- What is the specific research object?
- What is the core problem to be solved?
- What are the scope and boundaries of the research?
- What are the expected research outcomes?

**Examples**:
- "Research the interpretability of Transformer models"
- "Explore the application of few-shot learning in medical image analysis"
- "Analyze sample efficiency issues in reinforcement learning"


### Why (Why)

**Core Question**: Why is this problem important?

**Thinking Dimensions**:
- What is the academic value of this problem?
- What practical significance does solving it have?
- What are the limitations of current research?
- Why is now a good time to research this problem?

**Evaluation Criteria**:
- **Academic Importance**: Does it advance the field?
- **Practical Impact**: Does it solve real-world problems?
- **Timeliness**: Does it align with current research trends?
- **Feasibility**: Are there sufficient resources and technical support?

### Who (Who)

**Core Question**: Who are the target audience and stakeholders?

**Thinking Dimensions**:
- Who will care about this research?
- Who will use the research outcomes?
- Who will benefit from it?
- Who needs to collaborate with you?

**Stakeholder Classification**:
- **Academic**: Researchers in the field, reviewers, citers
- **Industry**: Product teams, engineers, decision makers
- **Users**: End users, affected groups
- **Collaborators**: Mentors, colleagues, interdisciplinary experts

### When (When)

**Core Question**: What is the time frame and context of the research?

**Thinking Dimensions**:
- What is the duration of the research?
- When is the optimal time to conduct this research?
- When will relevant technologies and data be available?
- When do you expect to produce results?

**Timeline Planning**:
- **Short-term** (3-6 months): Preliminary exploration, literature review, method validation
- **Medium-term** (6-12 months): Complete experiments, paper writing, submission
- **Long-term** (1-2 years): Systematic research, multiple papers, theoretical contributions


### Where (Where)

**Core Question**: What are the application scenarios and domains of the research?

**Thinking Dimensions**:
- What specific scenarios will the research be applied to?
- In which fields or industries is it valuable?
- Is geographic or cultural background important?
- Where will experiments be conducted?

**Application Scenario Classification**:
- **Academic Scenarios**: Theory validation, method comparison, benchmark testing
- **Industrial Scenarios**: Product development, system optimization, problem diagnosis
- **Social Scenarios**: Policy making, public services, social impact
- **Interdisciplinary**: Cross-disciplinary integration, technology transfer

### How (How)

**Core Question**: What is the preliminary research methodology approach?

**Thinking Dimensions**:
- What research methods or technical approaches will you use?
- What data and resources are needed?
- How will you validate your research hypothesis?
- What technical challenges are expected?

**Method Types**:
- **Theoretical Research**: Mathematical proofs, theoretical analysis, model derivation
- **Experimental Research**: Algorithm design, experimental validation, performance evaluation
- **Applied Research**: System development, case studies, user testing
- **Integrated Research**: Literature review, meta-analysis, systematic evaluation


## Usage Examples

### Example 1: Transformer Interpretability Research

**What**: Research the interpretability of attention mechanisms in Transformer models
**Why**: Improve model transparency, enhance user trust, meet regulatory requirements
**Who**: NLP researchers, model developers, regulatory bodies
**When**: 6-12 months, as Transformers are widely used but lack explanations
**Where**: Text classification, machine translation, question answering and other NLP tasks
**How**: Design visualization tools, analyze attention weights, conduct ablation experiments

### Example 2: Few-shot Learning Application in Medical Imaging

**What**: Explore the effectiveness of few-shot learning methods in medical image diagnosis
**Why**: Medical data annotation is costly; few-shot learning can reduce data requirements
**Who**: Medical AI researchers, radiologists, hospitals
**When**: 12-18 months, as few-shot learning technology matures
**Where**: X-rays, CT scans, MRI and other medical imaging diagnosis
**How**: Meta-learning, transfer learning, data augmentation, validated on real medical datasets


## Best Practices

### 1. Iterative Thinking

Do not expect to answer all questions at once. Do a quick pass through all 6 questions first, then iterate and refine repeatedly.

### 2. Priority Ranking

Adjust focus according to research stage:
- **Early Stage**: Focus on What, Why, Who
- **Mid Stage**: Focus on How, When, Where
- **Late Stage**: Comprehensively review all dimensions

### 3. Team Collaboration

Discuss 5W1H questions with mentors and colleagues to get multi-perspective feedback.

### 4. Documentation

Document your 5W1H analysis results as the foundation for your research proposal.
